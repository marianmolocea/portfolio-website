# Portfolio copywriting

The reader is usually a recruiter, hiring manager or another engineer with a minute to spare. They want to
know: who is this, what do they build, with what, and where can I see more. Optimise for: a reader who
skims gets the answer from the first screen; every sentence is specific enough that only this person
could have written it.

## The owner, in facts (use these, do not invent others)

Fullstack software engineer, 6 years of experience. Works with React, JavaScript, Go, Kubernetes and AWS.
Builds applications that control simulation models. Links: GitHub and LinkedIn only. Any other detail
(employer names, numbers, dates) must come from the owner; ask rather than make it up.

## Voice

Three traits, each with a guardrail:
- **Plain, not dull.** Everyday words and short sentences, but say something real.
- **Confident, not boastful.** State what they do and have done. Let facts carry the weight; no
  self-praise adjectives.
- **Friendly, not cute.** A light human touch is welcome (the site is meant to be a bit playful). Jokes,
  puns and exclamation marks are not needed.

## Rules

1. **Short sentences.** Aim for under 20 words. One idea per sentence.
2. **Everyday words.** "Use" not "leverage", "build" not "architect", "help" not "empower", "about" not
   "approximately".
3. **Concrete over abstract.** Name the thing: "a tool that starts and stops simulation runs on Kubernetes"
   beats "scalable cloud-native solutions".
4. **Active voice, first person.** "I build…", "I moved…". Not "Solutions were delivered…".
5. **Show with facts, not adjectives.** Replace "passionate", "expert", "highly skilled" with what was done.
   A number is the strongest fact, but only if the owner supplied it.
6. **Explain the niche once, simply.** "Simulation models" may be unfamiliar. One plain line on what that
   means in practice helps a non-specialist reader.
7. **Lead with the point.** The first sentence of each section should make sense on its own.
8. **Cut, then cut again.** The whole page should read in under two minutes. If a sentence can go without
   losing a fact, it goes.
9. **Skills are a list, not prose.** Group them simply (frontend, backend, infrastructure). No skill bars,
   percentages or star ratings; they mean nothing.
10. **Link labels say where they go.** "GitHub", "LinkedIn". Not "Click here", not "Let's connect!".
11. **No calls to email or a form.** The site has neither. If there is a closing line, point to LinkedIn.

## Words and patterns to avoid

Buzzwords: passionate, rockstar, ninja, guru, wizard, leverage, synergy, cutting-edge, innovative,
seamless, robust, best-in-class, world-class, results-driven, dynamic, empower, unlock, elevate, game-changer,
next-level, journey, cloud-native solutions, end-to-end solutions, digital experiences.

Patterns:
- Template openers: "Hi, I'm X, a passionate developer who loves turning ideas into reality."
- Lists of three vague adjectives: "clean, scalable and maintainable code".
- Claiming a value every engineer would claim: "I care about quality."
- Filler intensifiers: really, very, truly, extremely, incredibly.
- Jargon-stacking: three tech names in one clause where one would do.
- Emoji as decoration or as bullet icons.
- Exclamation marks.

## Before and after

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Passionate full-stack developer crafting innovative digital experiences. | I'm a fullstack engineer. I build software that runs and controls simulation models. |
| Leveraging cutting-edge cloud-native technologies to deliver scalable solutions. | I work in React and Go, and run things on Kubernetes and AWS. |
| I have extensive experience across the entire software development lifecycle. | Six years building web apps, from the screen people click on to the servers behind it. |
| Results-driven engineer with a proven track record of success. | (Delete it. Replace with one real thing they built, if the owner gives one.) |
| I am passionate about writing clean, maintainable, scalable code. | I like code that the next person can read and change without fear. |
| Feel free to reach out! Let's connect and build something amazing together! | Find me on GitHub and LinkedIn. |
| Expert in React (95%), Go (90%), Kubernetes (85%). | Frontend: React, JavaScript. Backend: Go. Infrastructure: Kubernetes, AWS. |

The "after" lines are shapes to follow, not final copy. Final wording should use the owner's own details.

## Meta text

- Page title: name plus role, nothing else.
- Meta description and share text: one plain sentence a stranger would understand, under 160 characters,
  following the same rules as the page.

## Checklist before accepting any copy

- [ ] Could another developer paste this line onto their own site unchanged? If yes, make it specific or
      cut it.
- [ ] No word from the avoid list; no exclamation marks; no emoji.
- [ ] Every sentence under about 20 words; reading level roughly that of a newspaper.
- [ ] Every claim is a fact the owner gave; nothing invented.
- [ ] Someone outside tech would understand what the owner does from the first screen.
- [ ] No email address and no invitation to email or fill in a form.
- [ ] Read aloud, it sounds like a person talking, not a press release.

A readability checker (several free ones exist, and some editors show a reading-grade score) answers "is
this harder to read than it needs to be?". Treat the score as a hint; the read-aloud test matters more.
